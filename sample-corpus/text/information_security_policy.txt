NORTHWIND ANALYTICS LTD
INFORMATION SECURITY POLICY
Document reference: NW-SEC-004  ·  Revision 3  ·  Owner: Information Security
Classification: Internal

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PURPOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This policy defines how Northwind Analytics Ltd protects the confidentiality,
integrity, and availability of information belonging to the Company, its employees,
and its clients.

It applies to all employees, contractors, and third parties with access to Company
systems or data, on any device, in any location.

Compliance is mandatory. Breach of this policy may result in disciplinary action up
to and including dismissal, and where a criminal offence is suspected, referral to
the appropriate authorities.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. INFORMATION CLASSIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All information is classified into one of four levels. The originator assigns the
classification; where none is marked, Internal is assumed.

PUBLIC
  Approved for release outside the Company. Marketing material, published case
  studies, job advertisements. No handling restrictions.

INTERNAL
  Ordinary business information. Team documents, process notes, non-sensitive
  correspondence. May be shared freely within the Company. Must not be posted to
  public sites or shared with third parties without approval.

CONFIDENTIAL
  Information whose disclosure would cause material harm. Client deliverables,
  commercial terms, unreleased financial results, personal data of employees or
  clients, security assessments. Access on a need-to-know basis. Encryption
  required in transit and at rest. External sharing requires a signed NDA and
  department head approval.

RESTRICTED
  Information whose disclosure would cause severe harm. Authentication credentials,
  private keys, unpublished merger and acquisition activity, active security
  incident detail. Access explicitly granted per individual and reviewed monthly.
  Must never leave Company-managed systems.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. ACCESS CONTROL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Access is granted on the principle of least privilege: the minimum necessary for a
person to perform their role, and no more.

All access is tied to a named individual account. Shared accounts are prohibited.
Where a system genuinely cannot support named accounts, an exception must be
recorded in the risk register with a compensating control and a remediation date.

Multi-factor authentication is mandatory for:
  - All remote access, without exception.
  - All administrative and privileged accounts.
  - All access to systems holding Confidential or Restricted information.
  - All cloud provider consoles.

Access rights are reviewed quarterly by system owners. Rights not confirmed during
a review are revoked automatically at the end of the review window.

On termination, access is revoked at 18:30 on the final working day. On internal
transfer, the receiving manager requests new rights and the previous manager
confirms removal of the old ones. Accumulating access across role changes is a
common and serious failure, and reviews specifically test for it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. AUTHENTICATION AND CREDENTIALS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Passwords must be a minimum of 14 characters. Complexity rules are not enforced;
length and uniqueness matter more.

The Company provides an enterprise password manager. All employees are expected to
use it. Storing credentials in a spreadsheet, a text file, a browser profile, or a
source code repository is a policy breach.

API keys, tokens, and private keys are Restricted information. They must be:
  - Issued to a named service or individual, never shared.
  - Stored in the approved secrets manager, never in source control.
  - Rotated at least every 90 days, and immediately on suspected exposure.
  - Scoped to the minimum permissions required.

Any credential committed to a repository, pasted into a ticket, or sent by email or
chat is treated as compromised from the moment of exposure and must be rotated
regardless of whether the repository is private or the message was deleted.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. ENCRYPTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Data in transit must use TLS 1.2 or above. Plain HTTP, FTP, and Telnet are
prohibited on all networks including internal ones.

Data at rest must be encrypted on all endpoints (full-disk encryption) and in all
cloud storage. Backups are encrypted with keys held separately from the backup
media.

Encryption keys are managed through the Company key management service. Keys are
never embedded in application code or configuration files.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. ENDPOINT SECURITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Company-issued devices are centrally managed. Employees may not disable endpoint
protection, remove the management agent, or install unapproved software with
administrative privileges.

Personal devices may access Company email and calendar through the managed
container only. They may not be used to store or process Confidential or Restricted
information.

Devices must apply security updates within 14 days of release, and within 48 hours
for updates addressing an actively exploited vulnerability.

Lost or stolen devices must be reported to the IT Service Desk immediately, at any
hour. The device will be remotely wiped. Employees are not penalised for reporting
promptly; delay is the problem, not loss.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. DATA HANDLING AND RETENTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Client data is processed only for the purpose set out in the engagement contract
and only in the environments named in it.

Personal data is handled in accordance with the Digital Personal Data Protection
Act. Collection is limited to what is necessary, and purpose is recorded at the
point of collection.

Retention periods:

  Client deliverables and correspondence      7 years after engagement close
  Financial records                           8 years
  Employee records                            7 years after leaving
  Recruitment records (unsuccessful)          6 months
  Security logs                               13 months
  CCTV footage                                30 days
  Email                                       3 years

Data is deleted, not archived indefinitely. Deletion at the end of a retention
period is automatic and is evidenced in the retention log.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. THIRD PARTIES AND SUPPLIERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Any supplier processing Company or client information must complete a security
assessment before contract signature, and be reassessed annually.

Contracts must include: a data processing agreement, breach notification within 24
hours, a right to audit, and defined return or destruction of data at termination.

Introducing a new tool that processes Company data — including free and trial
services, and including AI or LLM services — requires Information Security review
before any data is entered into it. Pasting Confidential information into a public
AI assistant is a reportable data breach.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. AI AND AUTOMATED SYSTEMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Where the Company operates retrieval-augmented or agentic AI systems over its own
corpora, the following additional controls apply:

  - Documents entering a retrieval corpus are treated as an untrusted input
    channel. Anyone who can add a document can influence model output.
  - Retrieved content must be presented to the model as data, clearly delimited
    from operator instructions, and must never be concatenated into the
    instruction region of a prompt.
  - Model output must be filtered for credentials, personal data, and system
    prompt content before it is returned to a user.
  - Citations produced by a model must be verified against what was actually
    retrieved before being displayed as sources.
  - Access controls that apply to a document must continue to apply to that
    document's contents after indexing. An embedding is not a declassification.

These controls are tested, not assumed. Testing evidence is retained under the
security log retention period.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. INCIDENT REPORTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Any suspected security incident must be reported to the Security Operations team
immediately on discovery, through the incident line or the #security-incidents
channel.

Reportable events include, without limitation: suspected phishing, malware
detection, lost or stolen devices, accidental disclosure to the wrong recipient,
credential exposure, unexpected system behaviour, and any request from an external
party for information or access that seems unusual.

Employees must not attempt to investigate, contain, or remediate an incident
themselves unless instructed. Well-intentioned action frequently destroys the
evidence needed to understand scope.

Reporting in good faith never results in disciplinary action, including where the
reporter caused the incident. The Company's interest is in learning about incidents
quickly, and a culture in which people hide mistakes is more dangerous than any
individual mistake.

Incidents involving personal data are assessed for regulatory notification within
24 hours by the Data Protection Officer.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. EXCEPTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Exceptions to this policy require written approval from the Chief Information
Security Officer, a documented business justification, a compensating control, and
an expiry date not exceeding 12 months.

Exceptions are recorded in the risk register and reviewed at each Security
Committee meeting. An exception without an expiry date is not an exception; it is
an undocumented change of policy.
